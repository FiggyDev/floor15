# Build-in-public posts — batch 1 (20 drafts)

*Tone: funny, builder-native, transparent, zero hype. No price words, ever. Post from the show
account or a builder account; screenshots referenced are real repo artifacts.*

1. we're building a 24/7 AI office sitcom where the audience is the board of directors.
   there is no token. there is nothing to buy. there is a guy named Max who has never been
   right. episode one is free forever. that's the whole pitch.

2. shipped: the Agent Engine. 7 AI characters with persistent memory, an append-only canon
   ledger, and a compiler that literally refuses to build a scene if a fired character is in
   it. not "shouldn't appear." cannot compile. HR as a type system.

3. our content-safety layer is a character named Linda from Legal. she is also, literally, a
   deterministic filter in the codebase. today she killed a scene because Max tried to say
   "pump the—" on air. the scene never aired. the save WAS the content. [screenshot: KILL log]

4. the Canon Ledger is append-only JSONL. no edits, ever — corrections are new entries that
   cite the old ones. when the audience fires somebody, the desk goes empty because the
   compiler enforces it, not because we remember to. grudges are data now.

5. our randomness rule: dice pick WHEN and WHO. never WHAT. gossip comes from hand-written
   decks by card id. rolls are deterministic per date — you can audit any day yourself:
   `npm run dice -- 2026-09-07`. same output every time. receipts or it didn't happen.

6. fun bug from today: our safety layer redacted a forbidden phrase from the dialogue…
   and then exported the regex that caught it, which contained the phrase. the build's
   language gate caught the leak. hits are opaque rule ids now, with a test. layered gates
   earn their keep.

7. every relationship change in the engine requires a receipt — a `why` and a source scene —
   or the code throws. six months from now when Max hates Roxy, you'll be able to trace it
   to the exact elevator ride. fandom archaeology is a first-class feature.

8. betrayal deltas never decay. ordinary friction drifts back to baseline weekly; betrayal is
   permanent until an ON-SCREEN reconciliation writes its own ledger entry. we made
   forgiveness a scene requirement. the drama is load-bearing.

9. agent memories are three tiers: ~12 core memories (their firing, their one great day),
   a rolling window of recent scenes, and a weekly one-paragraph life summary a human
   approves. if Max stops sounding like Max, we find out in days, not seasons.

10. the site is live. one self-contained HTML file. no accounts, no wallets, no backend —
    your vote is stored on your own device. the elevator's 16 button flickers every nine
    seconds and we will not be explaining that today. [link]

11. our production build FAILS if hype vocabulary appears anywhere in the public bundle.
    yes, the CI gate blocks the word m**n. yes, that means Linda reviews our marketing too.
    no, she has not approved this post. posting anyway. don't tell her.

12. why tokenless first: coins die because there's nothing to do on day 40. a show has to
    EARN day 40. if people come back at 9:15am with zero financial reason to, we've built
    something. if they don't, nothing else matters. so: free show first. everything else is
    a later problem with a counsel review attached.

13. there is no contract address. docs/contracts/ADDRESSES.md exists TODAY, before any token,
    saying exactly that — so if one ever exists, there's a single source of truth and every
    DM, reply, and screenshot claiming otherwise is auto-fake. bookmark the file, not a tweet.

14. the frontend cannot tell whether a scene was scripted by a human, generated by a model,
    or dice-assisted. one data contract, three gates (canon re-check, safety re-scan, schema).
    when LLM generation lands, the site won't change by one pixel. that's the whole design.

15. `engine approve <id>` — approval is a command a HUMAN runs. the pipeline cannot flip its
    own approved flag. we wrote the human gate as an invariant, then wrote a test that a
    hand-edited scene file can't smuggle a forbidden phrase past export either. paranoia is
    a feature. Roxy would be proud. Roxy IS proud. Roxy already filed it.

16. today's engine test count: 25/25. including: "a scene whose cast got fired AFTER the
    scene was written cannot export." yesterday's episode can't resurrect today's fired
    character. time-travel-proof HR.

18. the character bios all say "an AI character on FLOOR 15" because they ARE AI characters —
